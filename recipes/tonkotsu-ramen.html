TONKOTSU RAMEN

A steaming bowl of ramen featuring creamy, milky-white pork broth, springy noodles, and toppings such as sliced pork, soft-boiled egg, green onions, and seaweed.

Tonkotsu Ramen is a popular Japanese noodle soup made with a rich, creamy pork bone broth and served with springy noodles. It is commonly topped with tender sliced pork, soft-boiled egg, green onions, and seaweed, creating a rich and savory flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound split pig's trotters
  • 2 green onions
  • ½ yellow onion, unpeeled, quartered
  • 1 1-inch piece ginger, unpeeled
  • Sea salt, to taste
  • Soy sauce, to taste
  • 7 ounces fresh ramen noodles
  • 4 slices chashu pork (pork belly or loin cooked in soy sauce and mirin)
  • ¼ cup enoki mushrooms
  • 2 pieces menma (fermented bamboo shoots)
  • 2 pieces nori
  • 1 shoyu tamago (soy sauce egg) or soft boiled egg, halved
  • ¼ cup green onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tablespoon coarsely ground sesame seeds
  • Mayu (black garlic oil) or chili oil, to taste

Steps

  1. STEP ONE: Make the pork broth. Transfer the trotters to a large stock pot and cover with cold water. Bring to a boil over high heat, then remove from the heat and drain in a colander, discarding the liquid.
  2. STEP TWO: Using chopsticks, clean the bones under cold running water to remove any red or brown blood or organ pieces.
  3. STEP THREE: Transfer the cleaned bones to a clean stock pot.
  4. STEP FOUR: In a large cast iron skillet over medium-high heat, toast the green onions, yellow onion, and ginger, turning occasionally, until the aromatics are charred in places.
  5. STEP FIVE: Add charred aromatics to the stock pot with the cleaned bones and cover with cold water.
  6. STEP SIX: Bring to a boil over high heat, skimming off any scum that floats to the surface. Continue to boil and skim for 20 minutes, then cover and reduce to a simmer.
  7. STEP SEVEN: Continue simmering, adding enough water as needed to keep bones and aromatics covered, until broth is opaque, about 6 hours.
  8. STEP EIGHT: Uncover and bring to a boil. Let broth reduce to desired thickness.
  9. STEP NINE: Strain the broth through a fine-mesh strainer and refrigerate overnight.
  10. STEP TEN: When cool, bone broth should be very thick and gelatinous, and there should be a layer of fat on top of the broth.
  11. STEP ELEVEN: In a stock pot, bring pork bone broth and pork fat to a simmer over medium heat.
  12. STEP TWELVE: Season to taste with salt and soy sauce.
  13. STEP THIRTEEN: Meanwhile, bring a large pot of water to a boil.
  14. STEP FOURTEEN: Cook ramen noodles in boiling water according to package directions.
  15. STEP FIFTEEN: Divide the ramen noodles between two bowls and ladle the pork broth over the noodles.
  16. STEP SIXTEEN: Top each bowl with two slices of chashu pork, enoki mushrooms, and menma.
  17. STEP SEVENTEEN: Tuck a nori sheet in between the side of the bowl and an egg half.
  18. STEP NIGHTEEN: Sprinkle the bowls with green onions and sesame seeds, and drizzle with mayu and/or chili oil, as desired.
